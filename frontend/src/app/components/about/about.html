About NLP Scraper

NLP Scraper keeps you on top of tech news without the manual digging. It automatically pulls in the latest articles from TechCrunch, The Verge, and Ars Technica every hour, then reads each one for you — pulling out the people, companies, places, dates and figures it mentions, predicting its topic, and scoring its sentiment — so you can search, filter, and find what matters faster than skimming three separate sites.

What it does for you

  • Always up to date — new articles from all three sources are fetched automatically, hourly.
  • Search beyond the headline — full-text search covers article titles and body text, not just titles.
  • Filter by who, what, where, and tone — narrow results by person, organization, location, date, or quantity mentioned in an article, and by whether its overall sentiment is positive, neutral, or negative.
  • Know the topic and tone at a glance — every article is automatically classified (business, tech, sport, entertainment, or politics) and scored for sentiment, with a visual breakdown on its page.
  • Know the time commitment — each article shows an estimated reading time and word count on its page before you dive in.
  • Discover related coverage — every article links to up to 5 others that share its entities, topic, or tone.
  • Save articles for later — bookmark anything and come back to it anytime from the Saved page.
  • See the big picture — the Stats page charts article volume by topic, source, and sentiment, plus the most frequently mentioned people, organizations, and locations.

How to use it

  1. Browse — the Articles page lists the latest stories. Use the source chips (All / Techcrunch / Theverge / Arstechnica) to narrow the feed to one outlet, and the newest/oldest toggle to flip sort order.
  2. Search — use the search bar in the top bar to search across article titles and text.
  3. Filter — open Advanced filters on the Articles page to search by title, body text, sentiment, or by entity: persons, organizations, locations, dates & times, or quantities & percentages. Clicking any entity or sentiment segment on an article's page jumps straight to the filtered list.
  4. Read — click any article to see its full details: author, source, reading time, predicted topic, a sentiment breakdown chart, tags, extracted entities, related articles, and the full article text, plus a link back to the original.
  5. Save for later — click + Save for later on an article's page to bookmark it. Find everything you've saved anytime on the Saved page.
  6. Explore trends — visit the Stats page for a bird's-eye view: article counts by topic and source, the overall sentiment split, and the top mentioned people, organizations, and locations across every article.
